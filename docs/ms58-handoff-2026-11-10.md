## Mechanisms and hypotheses

1. **Pricing work diverges before correction or branching.** Cold C104 starts with the same 100 singleton columns and rounded objective 138171, then reaches 119325 in Kayros versus 119594 in f2 at the first 3100-column pool. An earlier crossed-dual replay retained each engine's pool despite exchanging the tiny initial dual differences. It found 713 differing paths under the shared heuristic 3000-solution limit. This implicates representation, extension, ordering or pricing semantics rather than the initial dual perturbation alone. It does not establish which engine is mathematically correct. See [first-divergence review](../outputs/ms58/dabia-20261110/c104-first-divergence-review/REVIEW.md).
2. **A generic baseline composition defect is established, but its C104 reachability is unknown.** Local checked-STL numerical fixtures show that f2's `PWLCompose` accesses an invalid index for an empty outer function or a constant inner value outside the outer domain. Kayros returns an empty function on those cases, and both agree on the valid constant case. The original f2 C104 logs contain finished root Exact calls and an empty terminal census, with no recorded infeasible RMP or unbranched fractional termination. None of that certifies pricing completeness. Preserve f2 as the comparison control while testing a separately identified diagnostic derivative.
3. **Correction dominance scans offer a concrete optimization target.** The a2 profiler completed 6 routing observations and exposed billions of U tests on C104, with over 99% rejected and about 97% sampled dominance time in scans. The 7d opportunity profiler also completed all 6 observations with no consistency failures. Its RC201 completed work matched 9d. Ten native gates passed and the G5K ILS core hash remained unchanged.
4. **Conservative correction blocks look more promising than a whole-bucket correction mask on C104.** In the final 7d trace, fixed 16-entry correction blocks could avoid 78.8996% of 2,870,440 sampled tests in returned Exact calls, and 79.0117% of 1,880,945 in unfinished call 45. Whole-bucket masks offered only 5.2645% and 6.1548%. These are opportunity counters, not executed pruning or speedups. Only 44 of 45 begun calls returned. Ordinary block opportunities were only 1.9014% and 3.0682%. RC201's correction groups averaged about 3 labels, so extra block maintenance is not justified there. See [final assessment](../outputs/ms58/dabia-20261110/u-filter-opportunity-gros-v1/analysis/dominance-index-final-20261110T114200Z/assessment.md) and its correction-block contract.
5. **Execution overhead and search work must be separated.** CPLEX batching is supported by repeated same-work comparisons. The opportunity profiler intentionally changes execution overhead and cannot support throughput claims. Packed ordinary metadata might improve cache locality despite few logical skips, but this remains untested. Do not substitute any WIP implementation for the validated 9d panel source.
6. **Handoff policies affect pricing work.** The final C104 panel supports retaining useful warm columns. The short pilot shows that their effect differs by instance. A blanket UB-only policy is not supported. The 5 s/15 s prefix tradeoff remains a full-solver choice, and startup-dominated closures cannot settle the n=100 objective.
7. **Preserve earlier correctness repairs and rejected attempts.** Canonical reductions removed observed 1-ULP ordering changes that replaced 4 of 10 branching candidates. The scalar-point repair recovered a missing column after a false optimality claim at 1847.239598 despite a feasible 1846.807019 witness. Safe correction repairs ownership and index ordering. Earlier normalization, scratch reuse and point-repair timing regressions remain evidence. Skipping PWL reconstruction merely because no apparent mutation occurred was rejected because epsilon merging is not idempotent. The eager-extension guard remains necessary.
